# The big problem: people can't close anything

## The short answer

"My thinking is scattered across tools" is true, but it's abstract, and research shows people rarely go looking for a tool to fix it. The problem people feel, and already install tools for, is concrete: **they can't close anything.** Tabs, AI chats and half-read pages pile up because closing them feels like losing the thought behind them.

So the product should lead with that: **close your tabs and AI chats without losing your train of thought.** Everything we designed (threads with evidence, modes, resume, briefs for any AI) stays. It sits behind that first win. This changes the front door, not the house.

## The insight: tabs are already how people save their train of thought

People already have a tool for keeping their train of thought: they leave tabs open. It's the most widely used memory aid on the web, and it does the job badly.

- In Carnegie Mellon's study of tab use, [more than half of the 103 people surveyed felt they couldn't close their tabs](https://www.sciencealert.com/tab-overload-is-a-common-problem-for-people-browsing-the-internet-survey-finds), because they worried about losing information that had taken effort to gather.
- [30% said they had a tab-hoarding problem, and a quarter said tabs were crashing or slowing their computers](https://www.fastcompany.com/90635776/the-twisted-psychology-of-browser-tabs-and-why-we-cant-get-rid-of-them).
- The researchers described a "black hole" fear: [once something went out of sight, people felt it was gone](https://www.cmu.edu/news/stories/archives/2021/may/overcoming-tab-overload.html). So they kept tabs open even after the number became unmanageable. The team's lead, Aniket Kittur, called tabs the number one tool people use for complex research and decisions, even though tabs do it poorly.
- AI chats are now a second pile. Extensions that add folders and search to ChatGPT have large followings: [Superpower ChatGPT says over 400,000 people use it](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/superpower-chatgpt/amhmeenmapldpjdedekalnfifgnpfnkc), and [AI Toolbox reports more than 40,000 active users](https://www.ai-toolbox.co/chatgpt-chrome-extension).

**Why this matters for downloads.** In a separate survey, people [spent about 4.4 hours a week on open-ended online research but mostly didn't use any tool for it, because they didn't think they needed one](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07206). Yet they do install relief for tabs: [OneTab has 2 million users](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/onetab/chphlpgkkbolifaimnlloiipkdnihall?hl=en-GB&pubDate=20260113). People don't download a "research tool." They download relief from a mess they can see. So our research tool should arrive as that relief.

**Why the relief is real, not just tidiness.**
- Unfinished goals keep interrupting your thoughts, and [making a concrete plan for one reduces those intrusive thoughts](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21688924/).
- Writing a quick [ready-to-resume plan](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/01/180116151600.htm) helps people let go of an interrupted task.

So "close and keep," with a one-line next step, removes the clutter and also gives your head permission to let go.

**Human-led tools can spread on their own.** Granola is an AI notepad that builds on the notes you type yourself. It [grew by word of mouth to a $1.5 billion valuation in March 2026](https://www.implicator.ai/granola-raises-125-million-at-1-5-billion-valuation-for-ai-notetaker/). Its pattern fits ours:
- a moment that happens every day (every meeting)
- a payoff you can see right away
- you stay in charge of the thinking

## The refined product

**Promise:** Close your tabs and AI chats. Keep your train of thought.

**The job it does:** When I've built up a pile of tabs and chats for something I'm in the middle of, I want to clear them without losing where my head was, so I can move on and come back later.

### The first minute

> **63 tabs open across 3 windows** · [Tidy up]
>
> **5 threads found**
> - **Choosing a bike:** 9 tabs (3 shops, 4 Reddit threads, 2 reviews)
> - **How attention works:** 11 tabs and 2 Claude chats
> - **Goa trip:** 7 tabs
> - **Thesis chapter 2:** 14 tabs
> - **Read later:** 22 tabs, most not opened in over a week · [Keep] [Let go]
>
> **Choosing a bike, where you were:** comparing three bikes. You were last reading an owners' thread about Bike B's brakes.
> *What were you trying to figure out?* (optional, one line, typed or spoken)
>
> [Close and keep] → **63 tabs → 4. Nothing lost.**

**What it reads:**
- It reads tab titles and addresses to group them.
- It reads page text only for the threads you save.
- It does this on your device, and only when you click.
- It skips banking, email and health sites by default.

### Every day

- **Close and keep.** When you're done with something for now, one click saves the thread and its "where you were" note. The note holds your question, what you'd worked out and your next step. The AI drafts it from the tabs and chats, and you can change it in one line.
- **Open where I was.** Reopening a thread shows the note, then restores the exact spots: the comment you were reading, the scroll position, the chat message, the video second.

  > **Choosing a bike** · closed 3 days ago
  >
  > Comparing three bikes. Last reading an owners' thread about Bike B's brakes. Your note: "brakes might be a dealbreaker, check A in rain."
  >
  > [Open where I was]
- **"You have a thread on this"** (optional). When you start searching a topic you already have a thread for, it reminds you where you were. Matching happens on your device. This follows [the survey authors' suggestion](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.07206) to treat earlier work as part of a longer journey, so it doesn't feel wasted.
- **AI chats count as tabs.** Add a chat to a thread, close it, and reopen it at the exact message. Continue in any AI with a brief.

### What grows from it

- **Capture as you go.** One click saves evidence and your take into a thread.
- **Views appear on their own.** Once a thread has enough evidence, it offers a view: a comparison table for a decision, a checklist for learning, or claims and sources for research. It asks what you're figuring out, not which mode you want.
- **Everything else from the proposal stays:** briefs for any AI, MCP, sync, recall and fading in learn mode.

**Use the word "thread," not "investigation."** "Your thread of thought" is how people talk, and it describes what we keep.

## Who downloads first

1. **People with too many tabs who feel overwhelmed by them.** Recruit people with ADHD as design partners, because tabs as a memory aid matter most to them. ADHD-focused tab tools already exist, which shows this community is actively looking for help.
2. **Heavy AI users whose chats pile up.** That means students, researchers and developers.
3. **A launch moment.** [Arc shuts down on 22 December 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/arc-browser-sunsets-december-22-as-the-browser-company-doubles-down-on-dia/), and many of its users cared about organized tabs. Arc's own founder said it was ["too different, with too many new things to learn, for too little reward"](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/27/the-browser-company-mulls-selling-or-open-sourcing-arc-browser-amid-ai-focused-pivot). The lesson: work inside the browser people already use instead of asking them to learn a new one.

**Paid later:**
- sync and hosted AI first
- then developers, using "close and keep" for coding-agent sessions (claude-mem's popularity shows the demand)

## The mission

**Think with AI and the web without losing your own thinking.**

The worry is widespread:
- RAND's survey of students
- Pew's survey of US adults
- the MIT study
- a Microsoft study finding that [more confidence in AI goes with less critical thinking](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/the-impact-of-generative-ai-on-critical-thinking/)

Use this story for the brand and press. Lead the download with the tab relief.

Chrome used to have an AI Tab Organizer that grouped tabs automatically, and [Google has since retired it](https://instinctive.app/chromesettings/TabOrganizerSettings). Grouping alone wasn't enough. Gemini summaries of tab groups are on the way, so "a summary of your tabs" will soon be free in Chrome.

Our edge is what Google won't build:
- your own reasoning
- AI chats from every company
- every browser
- your data on your device

## Risks

1. **"It's just another tab manager."** The category is crowded and mostly free, and OneTab is good enough for many people. **Handle it:** win on the reopen. The measure is "I was back in my head within a minute," not "tabs closed."
2. **Google and Microsoft give summaries away.** **Handle it:** lead with what they won't do: your reasoning, every company's AI chats, any browser, data on your device.
3. **Privacy.** Reading tab contents is sensitive, and [52% of AI Chrome extensions collect user data](https://about.fast.io/resources/best-ai-extensions-for-chrome-2026/). **Handle it:**
   - only read when you click
   - process on your device by default
   - skip sensitive sites
   - publish the code
   - ask for as few permissions as possible
4. **Bad grouping.** **Handle it:**
   - one-tap fixes
   - a "Read later" bucket and a "Let go" bucket
   - never close anything without showing what will happen first
5. **People close tabs and never come back.** Relief has value, but it won't keep people using the product. **Handle it:** the optional "you have a thread on this" reminder, plus tracking how many people reopen a thread within 7 days.
6. **The bigger vision gets lost.** **Handle it:** keep the hook small and the core the same. Threads hold evidence and reasoning, and views grow from use.

## Test it in three weeks

**Week 1: which promise pulls people in?** Put up three landing pages with the same design and the same budget:
- a. "Close your tabs and AI chats. Keep your train of thought."
- b. "Never re-explain yourself to an AI."
- c. "Think with AI without losing your own thinking."

Compare sign-up rates. Also post a 20-second before-and-after video ("63 tabs → 4, nothing lost") in a few communities where that's allowed.

**Week 2: does closing feel safe?** Run a hands-on "tab rescue" with 10 people who keep lots of tabs open:
1. They export their tab list. OneTab and most session managers can export titles and links.
2. You group the tabs and draft "where you were" notes with an AI prompt.
3. They close their tabs.
4. Three days later, they reopen one thread.

Measure:
- whether they closed most of their tabs
- how long it took them to get back in
- whether they'd be upset if the tool went away

**Week 3: does a thin version stick?** Build only:
- tidy up
- thread notes
- close and keep
- open where I was
- add an AI chat

Give it to 30 people. Track:
- tabs open before and after
- how many reopen a thread within 7 days
- how many are still using it after two weeks

**Decision rule.** Build version 1 if all three hold:
- most people close most of their tabs on day one
- at least 4 in 10 reopen a thread within a week and say it got them back in quickly
- the tab-relief headline beats the other two

## Changes to the proposal

1. **Lead problem:** "You can't close anything." Tabs and AI chats pile up because closing them feels like losing the thought.
2. **Hook and first run:** tidy up → threads → close and keep → "63 tabs → 4."
3. **User-facing word:** "thread" instead of "investigation."
4. **No mode question at setup.** Ask what you're figuring out. A thread offers a view once it has enough evidence. The decide view comes first, because comparison shopping is the most common kind of everyday research.
5. **AI chats are treated like tabs:** close and keep a chat, then reopen it at the exact message.
6. **First community:** people with too many tabs and heavy AI users, with people with ADHD as design partners. Arc's shutdown on 22 December is the launch moment.
7. **Mission line** for the brand and press: "Think with AI and the web without losing your own thinking."
8. **Phase 1 build:** tidy up, close and keep, resume, and AI chat capture. Evidence cards and the decide view follow right after.
9. **Paid expansion:** sync and hosted AI first, then coding-agent sessions for developers.
