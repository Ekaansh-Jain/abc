# The vision: the problems, why ours is better, and what we'll face

## 1. The idea

The bigger aim is to keep a person's train of thought intact when their thinking is spread across many tools and long stretches of time. Studying is the first case.

For each topic, it keeps:
- where you stopped and what you were in the middle of
- what you understood and how it clicked, with the exact source
- what's missing, what's wrong and what's fading
- how deep your goal needs you to go
- whether the topic is really done

It's built from every place you learn (AI chats, YouTube, GfG, Reddit, your own notes). Any AI you use can read it before helping. It doesn't teach, write study material or replace your notes. It does what notes can't.

## 2. The problems we're solving

### What I've run into as a student

**Starting a topic**
- I ask an AI what to study, and I can't tell whether its list is right or complete.
- I may not even know a topic exists, or how important it is.
- I don't know how deep to go in each topic.

**While learning**
- The AI explains several topics at once. I follow one, and the rest get left behind.
- When I'm confused and ask a side question in the same chat, it loses where it was and changes how it was teaching me.
- So I take that part to another AI to get it explained better, and now the explanation is split across two places.
- I get tired of chatbots and switch to a YouTube video. It finally makes sense, but that understanding stays in the video.
- I don't get one part of the video, go back to an AI, and the session limit runs out again.
- On GfG I build the reasoning myself, but when I open it a few hours later I don't know where I was or what I'd worked out.
- Something clicks on GfG, and then I write something different in my notes, or nothing at all.

**Notes**
- My notes become a mess, and I keep scrolling through chats to find things.
- I might write notes wrongly, badly, or not at all.
- Handwritten notes are sometimes not enough and sometimes too much.
- When a video changes my understanding, I have to rewrite my notes.
- After a few days, even my own notes don't make sense.
- AI-generated notes and summaries don't capture how I understood something.

**Coming back**
- I don't know where I left off.
- A summary of the topic doesn't put me back where my head was.
- After a few days, the chain of thought is gone.

**Remembering and checking**
- I don't know whether I studied something correctly.
- My understanding of a topic disappears, and I don't know why. Maybe it was shallow.
- AIs quiz me on things I've already done.
- Retaining, tracking, resuming and staying consistent are all still my job.

**The AIs themselves**
- Every new chat or new AI starts from zero, so explanations don't build on how I understood things.
- My context is stuck inside whichever AI I used.
- An app that switches models when the context runs out (like OpenRouter) doesn't fix any of this.
- Nothing connects it all, or knows me and what I did.

**Overall**
- Everything is fragmented, and all of these problems hit at once.
- Learning has many sides beyond the moment something clicks.

**What I don't want**
- Something that watches my computer or me all the time.
- Something closed, paid-only or invasive.
- Another summary tool, another wrapper or a static document.
- Guesses about what I understood that I can't see or correct.
- Checklists, timers or kanban boards. Those solve a different problem.

### Beyond studying

The same break in the train of thought happens wherever thinking is spread across tools and time.

- **Developers.** Claude Code's limit runs out mid-task, and ChatGPT gives unrelated answers because the context is in Claude. Why the code is the way it is, and what was already tried, lives in old sessions. New teammates need that understanding, and nobody wrote it down.
- **AI agents doing the work.** As agents do more of a project, you have to catch up on what they did and why, or you stop understanding your own work.
- **Work.** Many things happen at once, and a short break cuts the thread completely. Coming back after a meeting means rebuilding where you were and why. Handing work to a colleague loses the reasoning behind it.
- **Research and long projects.** A thesis spans months of papers, chats and notes, and the ideas you ruled out, along with why, get lost.
- **Decisions over weeks.** You choose a laptop, a college or a treatment across YouTube reviews, Reddit threads and AI chats, then forget what you concluded and why.
- **People who are interrupted constantly.** For people with ADHD, or anyone whose day is broken up, losing the thread is the main cost.
- **Using several AIs.** People now use more than one AI. Each holds a piece of your context, and none of them shares it.
- **Learning over years.** What you understood in one course should carry into the next. The matrix multiplication from a maths course should still be there when you learn LLMs a year later.
- **Teachers and mentors.** They can't see where each learner got stuck, which is exactly what they'd need to help.
- **Explanations that used to be shared.** Questions that once went to public forums now go privately to AIs, so an explanation that worked for one person no longer reaches the next person with the same confusion.

### Why no tool fixes it today

- **Context lives inside each tool.** Each chat, video, page and notebook holds its own piece.
- **Chats run in a straight line**, while thinking branches.
- **Notes freeze** the moment they're written.
- **AI memory stores facts about you** ("likes code examples") rather than where you are in a topic.
- **Summaries keep the content** and lose your reasoning.
- **Recording everything is invasive**, and a recording of what you saw doesn't tell you what you understood.
- **Model switchers move the chat**, and the new model still doesn't know you.
- **Productivity tools track tasks** rather than thinking.

## 3. What it is, and what it isn't

**It is**
- one record per topic (a "keeper") that holds your understanding of it
- a map of how topics and questions connect, with "you are here"
- a short brief any AI can read before helping you
- a done standard, after which the topic goes quiet
- a watch on what's fading, and why
- a home for your own notes, kept as you wrote them
- capture by one click, a session you start, or automatically, whichever you choose
- local, open source, and free at its core

**It isn't**
- a tutor or a course
- a study-material generator
- a notes app
- a quiz machine
- a chat app or a model router
- a checklist, timer or kanban board
- a screen recorder
- a static book or a summary
- a wrapper, because its core is the record of your understanding, which no AI model has

### What sets it apart

- **It starts from your understanding.** Other tools summarize sources, store chats or test you on material. Ours records what you understood, how it clicked and where you are.
- **It works with every tool.** Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, YouTube, GfG, Reddit and your notebook all feed one place, and every AI can read it.
- **Your notes stay yours.** Pages are attached to topics as you wrote them and never rewritten.
- **It brings back where your head was.** Coming back shows the question you were on and how far your reasoning got, instead of a summary.
- **It stays quiet when it should.** Checks happen only before a topic counts as done or when something is fading. Finished topics are left alone.
- **You choose how it learns about you.** One click, a session you start, or automatic.
- **It belongs to you.** Data stays on your device, the code is open, and the core is free.

### Compared with each alternative

**Handwritten and digital notes**
- Notes freeze at the moment you write them, while your understanding keeps changing.
- They don't know where you stopped, what's missing, what's wrong or what's fading.
- You can't hand them to an AI in a way that keeps it consistent with how you understood things.
- Ours keeps your notes and adds all of that around them.

**AI chats and their study modes (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini)**
- Each one only knows its own chats. [ChatGPT's study mode](https://chatgpt.com/features/study-mode/) remembers past sessions, but only ChatGPT's.
- Chats run in a straight line. Side questions land in the middle of the lesson, and long chats hit limits sooner.
- Their memory stores facts about you rather than your understanding of a topic.
- Ours sits across all of them and gives each one a short brief for the topic you're on.

**Gemini notebooks**
- [Strong at](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/gemini-finals-study-tips/) gathering sources in one place, making study guides, quizzes and audio overviews, and remembering your progress inside a notebook.
- They're built around what you upload into that notebook, inside Google's AI.
- Ours doesn't make study material. It keeps your understanding across every tool, Gemini included, feeds Claude and ChatGPT too, and stays on your device.

**Notes apps with AI ([Notability Learn](https://blog.notability.com/post/inside-notability-learn), [Goodnotes](https://support.goodnotes.com/hc/en-us/articles/7353756529551-Getting-Started-with-Study-Sets-and-Smart-Learn))**
- They turn your notes, PDFs and recordings into summaries, quizzes and flashcards.
- They only know what's inside their app.
- Ours also knows what happened outside your notes: the chat where it clicked, the second of the video, the question you left open.

**Save-and-summarize tools ([Recall](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/recall-summarizer-youtube/ldbooahljamnocpaahaidnmlgfklbben), [Dodo](https://dodogo.cc/))**
- They're good at pulling in YouTube, articles and PDFs, linking them and quizzing you. Dodo also separates "actively read" from "truly know it."
- They organize the content you saved.
- Ours organizes your understanding, with content attached as evidence at the exact spot.

**Tutors that track mastery ([Cognaa](https://cognaa.com/), [NoetaMind](https://noetamind.com/), [Knorion](https://knorion.com/))**
- They're the closest on understanding. They track mastery per concept and where you got stuck, and Cognaa remembers which explanations work for you.
- They mostly do it inside their own lessons and set subjects, and they do the teaching themselves.
- Ours tracks your understanding of anything, from wherever you learned it.

**Memory layers for AI ([Ontomaps](https://www.ontomaps.com/), [e-xo](https://e-xo.ai/), [knowledge-worker](https://github.com/rahulmranga/knowledge-worker))**
- They connect one memory to many AI tools:
  - Ontomaps shows "you are here."
  - e-xo lets unused memories fade.
  - knowledge-worker has you review what it wants to remember and requires a source for it.
- They store goals, decisions and facts, mostly for work and coding.
- Ours stores understanding: how it clicked, how deep it goes, how solid it is, and whether it's done.

**Chat-structure tools ([ChatGPT branching](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2025/09/chatgpts-new-branching-feature-is-a-good-reminder-that-ai-chatbots-arent-people/), [Offthread](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/offthread/ieekphhmaohoodalgaboiolcmmpoagmi?hl=en), [Branch Barber](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/branch-barber/pancajifpbhmoajlpbnmoigjbdeghifl), [GraphAqal](https://www.graphaqal.com/))**
- They fix the shape of chats with branches, side questions and maps of past conversations.
- They only work on chats.
- Ours keeps the chain of thought across all tools and ties it to topics.

**Screen-recording memory (Rewind and similar)**
- They capture everything by recording your screen. [Rewind was shut down](https://9to5mac.com/2025/12/05/rewind-limitless-meta-acquisition/) after Meta bought the company in December 2025.
- A recording of what you saw doesn't tell you what you understood, and it costs privacy.
- Ours only learns through your clicks, sessions you start, or automation you turn on.

### What no one else combines

Each product above has one or two of the pieces. None of the ones I found has all of these together:
- understanding tracked per topic
- built from all your sources and AIs
- readable by every AI
- a done standard
- a real way to resume
- your notes kept as they are
- data that stays yours

## 5. Problems we'll face

### The ones that matter most

1. **Judging understanding.** If it marks things understood too early, everything built on top is wrong. If it doubts you too often, it nags.
2. **Getting at the sources.** AI apps don't give outside tools an easy way to read your chats, and websites change.
3. **Big companies adding pieces.** Google, OpenAI and Anthropic keep improving memory and study features inside their own apps.
4. **Becoming a habit.** Students try study tools and drop them, so the value has to show up in the first week.

### Product

- **What counts as a topic.** Topics overlap and nest ("attention" contains "queries and keys"), and bad boundaries make the map messy. **Handle it:** start from the plan the student already has. Allow merging, splitting and moving topics with one tap, and learn from those corrections.
- **Filing things in the wrong place.** A question or a video can land under the wrong topic. **Handle it:** show where each thing went and make moving it one tap.
- **Recording the wrong click.** It might save the wrong explanation as the one that worked. **Handle it:** show it, let the student correct it, and only ask when unsure.
- **Asking too much.** An AI quizzing you on things you've already done is the problem you named yourself. **Handle it:** a strict rule that checks happen only before a topic counts as done or when it's fading.
- **Wrong flags on notes.** Telling a student their note is wrong when it isn't destroys trust quickly. **Handle it:** flag only with a cited source, and stay conservative.
- **Knowing the right depth.** Depth depends on the goal (an exam, an interview, building something, curiosity), and an AI's sense of depth can be off. **Handle it:** ask for the goal once, use the syllabus or past papers when they exist, and let the student adjust.
- **Learning that never gets in.** Lectures, textbooks and conversations with friends happen away from any app. **Handle it:** photos of notes, quick voice notes, and showing gaps honestly instead of pretending to know.
- **Empty on day one.** A new install knows nothing. **Handle it:** import past ChatGPT and Claude history to seed topics, and let the student start from a plan.
- **Drifting into a summary or notes app.** There will be steady pressure to add AI summaries and note-taking, which is exactly where Notability, Goodnotes and Gemini are strongest. **Handle it:** make it a rule never to rewrite notes, and keep generated study material out of the headline features.
- **Where it lives.** It has to appear in the browser, on the phone and inside AI apps without cluttering any of them. **Handle it:** keep it invisible while you work, with a one-line "you are here" bar and the full map on demand.
- **Subjects work differently.** Maths and coding are about solving problems, languages about practice, and history about connected events. **Handle it:** let a topic's parts be concepts, problem types or skills.

### Technical

- **No easy access to AI chats.** ChatGPT and Claude let you export your history, but there's no simple way for another app to read new chats as they happen. Browser extensions break when those sites change. Some AI apps let outside tools plug in through connectors, but support differs by app and plan. **Handle it:** support several routes (an extension, connectors and imports), so losing one doesn't break the product.
- **Writing a good brief.** What gets handed to another AI has to be short enough not to eat its limit and complete enough to keep it consistent with how you understood things. **Handle it:** one brief per topic, tested on real handoffs.
- **Pages and videos change.** Articles move, and YouTube transcripts aren't always available. **Handle it:** store the link, the timestamp and your own words, so the record survives when the page changes.
- **AI costs money.** Organizing the map and judging understanding both need model calls. **Handle it:** small local models for routine work, bring-your-own-key, and a paid hosted option.
- **Local data on several devices.** Students switch between phone and laptop. **Handle it:** start on one device, then add end-to-end encrypted sync.
- **Knowing whether its judgments are right.** It needs a way to tell whether "understood," "fading" and "done" are accurate. **Handle it:** track how often students correct it, and how often a "done" topic fails a later check.

### Market and business

- **Big companies.** Gemini notebooks already remember progress, and ChatGPT's study mode remembers past sessions. Both will improve. **Handle it:** be the things they won't be: neutral across rival AIs, kept on the user's device, and built around understanding rather than generated material.
- **Habit.** Students drop tools that need a new routine. **Handle it:** ask for no new routine and show up where they already study. Make the first win obvious, such as opening the laptop and being exactly where you were, or switching from Claude to ChatGPT without explaining everything again.
- **Who pays.** Students have little money and expect free. **Handle it:**
  - a free open core (local models or your own key)
  - paid convenience (sync, hosted models)
  - later, institutions such as coaching centres, universities, bootcamps and companies
- **Explaining it.** "It holds your understanding" is abstract. **Handle it:** lead with concrete moments like the ones above.
- **Too broad too early.** The idea fits students, developers and work, and doing all of them at once spreads the effort thin. **Handle it:** start with students who learn with AI, and keep the core general so the developer and work versions can reuse it.
- **Privacy and trust.** A record of what someone understands, and doesn't, is personal, and work versions may touch confidential material. **Handle it:** local by default, open code, and nothing stored that the user can't see and delete.
- **Small competitors moving fast.** Ontomaps, Dodo, NoetaMind and others are early and could grow into this space. **Handle it:** move fastest on the part none of them has, which is understanding across tools.

### Learning design

- **Feeling you understand isn't the same as understanding.** Right after something clicks it feels solid, and often isn't yet. **Handle it:** "done" needs one proof, such as explaining it or solving a problem.
- **Leaning on it too much.** If the system remembers everything, students may skip the effort that makes things stick. **Handle it:** the system keeps the state, but recall stays the student's job, so questions come before answers.
- **Too many open threads.** Showing every parked question can overwhelm. **Handle it:** surface only what matters for the current goal.

### Wider problems

- **Every AI takes context differently.** Chat apps, coding agents (Claude Code, Cursor, Codex) and future agents each accept outside context in their own way, and support for connectors varies. **Handle it:** keep the record in an open, plain-text format, use connectors where they exist, and fall back to a brief you can paste.
- **An open format nobody else reads.** Openness only helps if other tools use it. **Handle it:** make the product useful on its own first, then publish the format and make it easy to read.
- **Agents changing your work.** If agents act for you without recording why, you lose understanding of your own project. **Handle it:** let agents add to the record with your approval, and give you a catch-up view.
- **Teams and companies.** Shared records raise questions about who sees what, and many companies forbid sending work content to outside AI services. **Handle it:** separate personal and shared spaces, allow self-hosting, and let companies choose their own AI models.
- **Teachers and parents.** Seeing where a learner is stuck helps, but it can feel like surveillance. **Handle it:** the learner decides what's shared and can stop sharing at any time.
- **Children's data laws.** India's data protection law [requires verifiable parental consent for children's data and bars tracking and behavioural monitoring of children](https://ohrh.law.ox.ac.uk/childrens-educational-data-and-apaar-id-a-test-for-indias-privacy-regime/), and it treats anyone under 18 as a child. In the US, COPPA sets rules for children under 13. A tool that tracks understanding could count as monitoring. **Handle it:** start with adults (18 and over), and get legal advice before serving school students.
- **People who get interrupted constantly.** People with ADHD may benefit most, but a cluttered map or frequent prompts would hurt them most too. **Handle it:** calm defaults, little on screen, and nothing that demands attention.
- **Phones and patchy internet.** Many students study mainly on phones, sometimes with limited data. **Handle it:** a light mobile app that works offline and syncs later.
- **Explanations in other languages.** What clicked might have been a Hindi YouTube lecture or a mix of languages. **Handle it:** keep each explanation in the language it worked in, and let briefs ask the AI to explain the same way.
- **Schools wary of AI.** Some institutions see AI tools as cheating aids. **Handle it:** make it clear that it doesn't write answers or assignments. It keeps track of your own learning.
- **Years of records.** Over years, records grow large and messy, and old topics need merging. **Handle it:** periodic tidying, merging and archiving that the user approves.
- **Proving it helps.** Without evidence, it's one more app making claims. **Handle it:** measure simple things, such as how long it takes to get back into a topic, how often you re-explain yourself to an AI, and how many "done" topics pass a later check.
- **Shared explanations and privacy.** Letting an explanation that worked for one person help others is valuable, but it must never leak anyone's private record. **Handle it:** sharing is opt-in, per topic, and strips everything personal.
